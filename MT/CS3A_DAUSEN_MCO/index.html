The Weight of Wings

A Story of Ambition, Breakdown, and Rebuilding

Chapter I

Ascent of Icarus

August 10, 1996 - a date that marked the beginning of an ascent I mistook for endless flight. From my earliest memories of my childhood, dictionaries and encyclopedias about various subjects weren't just books, they were doors leading to a vast repository of knowledge and I went through those doors like a fish out of water returning to the vast sea. The people around me, in turn, showered my curiosity with praise. 'Smart,' they'd say, a label that clung to me like a second skin. It was a comfortable identity, one that fueled my academic drive. From when I was awarded 'Most Outstanding' in preschool to the 'First Honor' ribbons that adorned my chest in my elementary years, the praises piled up. I was a consistent victor, a representative of my school and district in a relentless parade of academic competitions. The culmination? Graduating as class valedictorian of Galimuyod Central School Batch 2009, proof of my perceived brilliance.

High school was no different. The 'Academic Awardee' certificate became a constant companion, culminating in a top-eight finish in Saint Joseph Institute Batch 2013. Even the occasional lectures from teachers about my 'laziness' - a critique I dismissed with a shrug, considering my consistently high grades - couldn't puncture the balloon of my self-assuredness. I was soaring. The college entrance exams were merely formalities. Saint Louis University and Adamson University, both with engineering as my first choice, welcomed me with open arms. The remark 'you may enroll in any program of your choice' echoing like a confirmation of my inherent superiority. Even across continents, my reputation preceded me. My mother, working in the UK, recounted stories of her wards, two children four years older and my age, who were always amazed by my academic feats. They saw a child who, despite the absence of a mother's constant presence, achieved seemingly impossible heights.

Looking back, I see the threads of hubris woven into the fabric of my success. The praises, the awards, the effortless achievements - they painted a picture of limitless potential, a flight without end. I was Icarus, basking in the warmth of my perceived brilliance, unaware of the wax melting beneath the sun. The ascent felt effortless, the sky seemed infinite, and the fall, unthinkable.



Chapter II

Shattered Feathers

The wings that once carried me with such confidence began to falter the moment I stepped onto the campus of Adamson University. The city's pulse, so different from the quiet rhythm of my province, was both exhilarating and disorienting. Yet, I adapted, a testament to my resilience, or so I thought. The first two years were a deceptive calm, a period of passable grades masking a growing unease. The cracks, though small, were forming. My chosen major, Civil Engineering, didn't feel like a calling and more like a random turn on a map. Then came Engr. Bonifacio, his words echoing in the lecture hall, 'Students fail because they lack focus, because they are young or they chose the wrong major.' His words, like a seed, took root in my mind, a premonition of my own collapse. Becoming an officer of the Physics Society of Adamson University provided a fascination with other engineering disciplines and only amplified the doubt.

By my fourth year, the facade crumbled. The praise of professors felt empty, undeserved. I was an imposter, a fraud. My motivation, once a roaring flame, flickered and died. Classes were abandoned, replaced by a desperate hold to the illusion of student leadership. My priorities warped, twisted by a growing sense of inadequacy. The inevitable fall came swiftly. Dropped classes, failed subjects, and the cold advice to transfer or shift to a 'less demanding' program. My world shattered. The weight of my mother's sacrificed tuition, the sense of being a burden, pressed down on me like a physical weight. I was no longer the golden child, but a financial liability. My organization's former adviser, a lifeline for my drowning self, offered a way out, a chance to rebuild. But fear, the fear of failure, the fear of disappointing him, paralyzed me. I declined his offer, giving flimsy excuses, unable to trust myself to fly again.

My cousins' well-meaning advice, to just finish college, led me to the Technological Institute of the Philippines - Manila by 2017. A new start, a fresh environment, but the same underlying doubt. The initial burst of success was short-lived. The lack of connection, the feeling of being an outsider, hastened my descent. The pressure, both internal and external, became unbearable. Self-harm, a desperate cry for release, was only averted by a brother's tearful plea. I stopped going to school and went home to the province the following year, 2018, to take a year off and rest my weary mind. It was a retreat to the province, a 'rest' that was anything but. The constant questions and the unspoken judgment of the people who once rained praises on me fueled my shame.

Another transfer, another attempt to escape the inevitable led me to the University of Northern Philippines in 2019, a desperate hope for redemption. The initial success was a cruel mirage. Then came a certain professor who I'll just call Mr. UP Diliman. Though I can't recall his name because I've blocked him from my memories, his condescending attitude and his attempts to belittle my past remain unforgettable. He was the final straw. The weight of incompetent professors, the toxic environment, the feeling of being constantly singled out, crushed me. I abandoned my studies that same year, the shattered feathers of my ambition scattered and lost.



Chapter III

Forging New Wings

The abrupt halt of my academic journey coincidentally happened with a global stillness, the Covid19 pandemic. For two years, the world held its breath, and so did I. Restricted to my home, I was left with nothing but time - time to reflect, examine my mess of a past, and confront the remains of my ambition. It was a time of enforced introspection, a silent trial in which the fragments of my former self were slowly reshaped. Those two years, though marked by isolation and uncertainty, became an unexpected catalyst for change and realizations.

The first realization was an understanding of the crushing weight of self-imposed pressure. I had driven myself to the edge of a cliff, fueled by an insatiable need for achievement, and ignoring the fundamental need for rest. Burnout, once an abstract concept, became a tangible reality, a stark reminder that even the most driven spirit needs rest. The second realization was about the temptations of external validation. The praises, expectations, and constant affirmations became like drugs, intoxicating substances that warped my perception of self-worth. I learned to detach my identity from the opinions of others, to find value in my own journey, independent of their gaze. The final, and perhaps most important, realization was the pointlessness of comparison. The world around me, with its endless parade of success stories, had become a mirror reflecting my own perceived failures. But in the stillness of those two years, I learned to silence the noise, to embrace my own pace. The paths of others, though alluring, were not mine. I would find my own way, at my own time, taking each step with deliberate intention.

When the world began to stir again by 2022, I did too. I returned to the academic world, but on my own terms. I enrolled in North Luzon Polytechnic State College, choosing Computer Science as my major, a field that resonated with my newfound sense of self. Some may have questioned my decision, labeling it a “waste” of years and potential. But I had learned to prioritize my own happiness, to value personal fulfillment over external validation.

Looking back, I see the scars of my past, the fragments of my former self. But I also see the strength forged in reflection. The mistakes, the failures, the painful lessons - they were not a waste, but an integral part of my journey. They were the raw materials from which I forged new wings, wings that would carry me not towards a false illusion of limitless flight, but towards a genuine sense of purpose and self-acceptance.
